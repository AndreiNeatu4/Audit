EU Accessibility Checker

Checks a web page against the European Accessibility Act — Directive (EU) 2019/882 (CELEX 32019L0882). Requirements are grouped under the directive's four principles — perceivable, operable, understandable, robust — and verified via the harmonised standard EN 301 549 → WCAG 2.2 Level A + AA (the direction of ETSI's draft EN 301 549 V4.1.0, ahead of the WCAG 2.1‑based standard currently in force).

Article 15 (Presumption of conformity): a website conforming to EN 301 549 is presumed to conform to the directive's accessibility requirements (Annex I).

How to read these results

  • Pass An automated test found no problem for this requirement.
  • Fail An automated test detected a violation. Click the row for the exact elements.
  • AI-suggested No rule-based test could check this — a model looked at a screenshot and the page's HTML and gave a non-binding best guess. This is never a verified result.
  • Manual review This requirement can't be fully judged by a machine — a person must verify it.

Automated testing runs two independent engines — axe-core and HTML_CodeSniffer — against every page, since different engines catch different issues; a finding from either counts. Together they can reliably verify roughly 30–50% of WCAG criteria — some further criteria may carry an AI-suggested hint when enabled, but WCAG itself defines many criteria (is this text meaningful, is this the right heading, etc.) in terms of human judgement that no automated tool, including AI, can certify. A clean automated report is not a guarantee of legal conformance: Article 15 of the directive grants only a presumption of conformity to sites that meet EN 301 549 in full, so “Manual review” and “AI-suggested” items still need a human to sign off for compliance with Directive (EU) 2019/882 (CELEX 32019L0882).
